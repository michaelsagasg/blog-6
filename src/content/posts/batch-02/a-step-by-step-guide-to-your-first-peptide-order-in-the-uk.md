## Five steps from research to delivery

A first UK peptide order goes through five steps worth doing deliberately: vet the seller, verify the batch-specific COA, place a smaller starting order, check the delivery on arrival, and confirm everything matches before you consider a repeat order. Rushing past any of these steps is where most avoidable problems happen.

### Why the process matters more on a first order

You have the least information about a new seller before your first order and the most leverage to walk away if something doesn't check out. That balance flips once you've paid, which is exactly why the verification work is worth doing upfront rather than after.

## Step one: vet the seller

Search the seller's stated company name on Companies House and confirm an active entry with a plausible incorporation date. Check the delivery policy states UK dispatch and a tracked service. Look for independent reviews, not just on-site ones, spread across a reasonable timeframe rather than clustered together.

### A quick pass, not a deep audit

This step should take about ten minutes. You're looking for obvious gaps (no company found, no delivery policy at all, no independent reviews anywhere) rather than an exhaustive investigation at this stage.

## Step two: verify the COA

Check the product listing shows a COA naming the current batch number, test date, testing method (HPLC-UV), and a purity figure. If it isn't shown, email and request it before paying. Confirm the batch number matches what the listing states, and that the purity figure looks like a plausible measurement rather than a suspiciously round number.

### Ask a direct question at this stage too

Use this step to also test customer service: ask something specific, like the exact dispatch timeframe for your postcode, and see how quickly and specifically they respond.

## Step three: place a smaller starting order

Even if a larger quantity offers a better per-unit price, order a single vial first. This limits your financial exposure while you evaluate the seller's actual process rather than just their claims.

### What you're testing at this stage

Dispatch speed against what was promised, the tracked delivery service actually used, and how order confirmation and any update emails are handled.

## Step four: check the delivery on arrival

When the parcel arrives, check the packaging condition and whether the ice pack still had cooling capacity left, compare the actual delivery time to what was stated at checkout, and check the batch number on the vial label against the COA you were given.

### What to do if something doesn't match

Report any mismatch, a warm parcel, a different batch number, a missing COA, to the seller promptly, referencing their stated returns policy. How they respond to this first real test is highly informative for any future orders.

## Step five: decide on repeat orders

A smooth first order, accurate documentation, on-time delivery, everything matching as promised, is a reasonable basis to order again, potentially at a larger quantity. Any unresolved problems at this stage are worth weighing seriously before ordering from that seller again.

### A seller built for this process

A seller such as Enzo Meds provides batch-numbered COAs and stated delivery terms upfront, which streamlines steps one and two and leaves less to verify manually before you place a confident first order.

## How this looks in practice, start to finish

For a typical first order, expect to spend 20-30 minutes on steps one and two before ordering, then a 1-2 working day dispatch window and a 2-4 working day delivery window for step three. Step four takes a few minutes once the parcel arrives, and step five is really just an honest reflection on how the previous four steps went.

### Building this into a habit

The first time through, this process can feel like a lot of extra effort for a single order. Once it becomes a habit, most of it becomes quick and automatic, and it consistently pays off by catching the small number of sellers genuinely worth avoiding before you've committed any money to them.

## In short

Vet the seller, verify the batch-specific COA, start with a smaller order, check everything on arrival, then decide on repeat orders based on how the whole process actually went. Following these five steps deliberately is what turns a first order into a genuinely informed decision rather than a guess.
