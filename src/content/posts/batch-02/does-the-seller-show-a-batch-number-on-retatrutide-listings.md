## Check for the batch number before anything else

A trustworthy retatrutide listing shows a specific batch or lot number, something like RT-250714-02, either on the product page itself or on the attached COA, and that number matches what's printed on the vial you receive. If a listing never mentions a batch number anywhere, there's no way to connect any testing claim to the actual product being shipped to you.

### What a batch number actually proves

The batch number is the link between the COA and the physical vial. Without it, a seller could show you a genuine-looking lab report for a completely different, possibly better, batch than the one they're actually sending. It's a small detail that does a disproportionate amount of the verification work.

## What a genuine batch number looks like

Real batch numbers usually follow a consistent format the seller reuses across their catalogue, often combining a product code, a date, and a sequence number, for example RT-250714-02 (product, date, sequence). They change between restocks, since a new manufacturing or repackaging run gets a new batch. A number that never changes across months of restocks is a sign it isn't tracking real batches at all.

### Where it should appear

Look for the batch number in three places: on the product listing text, on the COA document, and ideally printed on the vial label itself as shown in any product photos. All three should match. A batch number that only appears on the COA and nowhere else on the listing is weaker than one stated consistently across all three.

## What it means if the batch number is missing

A retatrutide listing with no batch number anywhere, and no COA offered even on request, is not verifiable at all. That doesn't automatically mean the product is fake, but it does mean you're relying entirely on the seller's general reputation with no way to check a specific claim against a specific vial.

### Ask directly if it's not shown

If the batch number isn't visible on the listing, email and ask which batch is currently being shipped and request the matching COA. A seller with a genuine per-batch process can usually answer this within a day or two, often with the batch number and COA attached directly in the reply.

## Why this matters more for retatrutide specifically

Retatrutide is newer to the UK market than tirzepatide, and fewer sellers have an established, multi-batch track record with it. That makes the batch-to-COA link more important here, since there's less prior information available to fall back on if the documentation itself is thin.

### A working example

A listing like Enzo Meds shows the batch number on the retatrutide product page itself, matching the number on the attached COA, which is the standard worth checking any other listing against.

## What a changing batch number over time tells you

If you order from the same seller more than once, compare the batch numbers across orders. A different, sequential-looking batch number on a repeat order is a good sign of genuine, ongoing inventory turnover. The same batch number appearing months apart is worth asking about directly, since genuine stock of a specialised peptide like retatrutide doesn't typically sit unsold for that long at an actively selling business.

### Keep your own record

It's worth keeping a simple note of the batch number and COA for each order you place. If an issue comes up later, whether with the product itself or in a wider conversation about a particular batch, having your own record makes it much easier to check whether it affects you specifically.

## In short

Look for a batch number on the listing, confirm it matches the COA, and check that the number changes between restocks rather than staying static for months. A listing that can't show you a batch number at all, even on request, hasn't given you anything concrete to verify.
