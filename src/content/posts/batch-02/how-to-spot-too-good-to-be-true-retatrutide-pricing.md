## Know the realistic range first

As of 2026, established UK sellers commonly price retatrutide around £90-£140 for a 5mg vial and £160-£230 for a 10mg vial. Pricing meaningfully below that range, particularly from a new or undocumented seller, is one of the more reliable signs the product isn't what it claims to be, rather than a genuine bargain worth taking advantage of.

### Why retatrutide pricing sits where it does

Retatrutide is a larger, more complex peptide to manufacture and test than tirzepatide, and genuine batch-specific testing (HPLC-UV analysis through a real lab) adds real cost per batch. Sellers doing this properly build that cost into the price. A price that doesn't reflect it is a price that likely skipped it.

## What a too-good-to-be-true price often means

Underpriced retatrutide is disproportionately likely to be underdosed (less actual peptide per vial than stated), a different or cheaper substance entirely, or old stock relabelled without new testing. None of these are things a price alone can prove, but a price well outside the normal range is a reasonable trigger to check the COA and company details more carefully than you otherwise might.

### The "introductory offer" pattern

A steep, time-limited "launch discount" on a brand-new site with no trading history and no COA is a common pattern worth extra scrutiny. A modest 10-20% first-order discount from an otherwise well-documented seller is normal marketing; a 60-70% discount from an unverified new site is a different thing entirely.

## Compare like for like

Check the exact strength and quantity being sold before comparing price. A listing advertising a low price per vial that turns out to be a 2mg vial, compared against another seller's 5mg vial, isn't actually cheaper once adjusted for strength. Always compare price per mg, not just the headline number.

### Bundle and bulk pricing

Legitimate bulk discounts exist, buying three vials at once is often modestly cheaper per unit than buying one. But a bulk discount so steep it undercuts the realistic per-vial range even at single-unit pricing is worth the same scrutiny as any other unrealistically low price.

## What to do when you spot low pricing

Before dismissing a low-priced listing outright, check whether it's backed by a batch-specific COA, a findable Companies House entry, and reasonable delivery terms. If all three check out, the lower price might reflect a genuinely leaner operation. If none of them check out, the low price is the least surprising part of the listing.

### A benchmark for realistic pricing

A listing such as Enzo Meds, priced within the typical UK range and backed by a batch-numbered COA and verifiable company details, is a useful benchmark for what realistic retatrutide pricing looks like when it's paired with genuine documentation.

## Factor in what genuine testing actually costs

Independent HPLC-UV testing through an accredited lab typically costs a seller a real, per-batch fee, plus the cost of the reference standards and staff time involved in preparing samples. A seller genuinely doing this for every batch has a cost floor built into their pricing that an untested or minimally tested operation simply doesn't have, which is a big part of why the price gap between genuine and low-quality retatrutide can be so large.

### Ask what's included in the price

Some sellers bundle cold-chain packaging, tracked delivery, and testing costs into a single headline price, while others charge extra for expedited or better-insulated shipping. Comparing the full, final price at checkout, not just the listed unit price, gives a more accurate picture when judging whether a price is genuinely low or just structured differently.

## In short

Know the typical UK range, roughly £90-£140 for 5mg and £160-£230 for 10mg as of 2026, and treat pricing well below that as a reason to check the COA and company registration more carefully, not as a straightforward bargain. Genuine testing and sourcing has a real cost, and pricing that doesn't reflect it usually means something was skipped.
