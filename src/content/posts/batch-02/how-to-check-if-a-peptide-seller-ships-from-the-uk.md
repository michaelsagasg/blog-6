## Check the stated dispatch location

A seller shipping from within the UK should state this clearly on the delivery or shipping policy page, often alongside a specific dispatch location or warehouse region. Delivery timeframes for genuine UK-based dispatch are typically 2-4 working days using Royal Mail Tracked or a UK courier. If delivery estimates run to 1-3 weeks or mention customs clearance, the parcel is very likely coming from overseas regardless of what the site's branding suggests.

### Why UK dispatch matters here specifically

Peptides shipped internationally face customs risk, since import of research chemicals and peptides into the UK can be delayed, inspected, or seized depending on how the parcel is declared. A UK-based seller shipping domestically avoids this entirely, which is part of why UK dispatch is worth confirming rather than assuming from a .co.uk domain alone.

## A .co.uk domain isn't proof of UK dispatch

A UK-registered domain name only confirms the domain registration, not where the actual product ships from. Some sites present as UK-based in branding and domain while fulfilling orders from an overseas warehouse. Check the delivery policy text itself, not just the domain extension or currency shown at checkout.

### Check the returns address too

If a returns policy states an overseas address for sending items back, that's a strong indication the actual operation isn't UK-based, even if the customer-facing site uses British spelling and pound sterling pricing throughout.

## Look at the delivery service named

UK domestic dispatch usually names Royal Mail (Tracked 24, Tracked 48, or Special Delivery) or a UK courier such as a named next-day service. A site naming an international postal service, or simply saying "tracked shipping" with no UK-specific service named, is worth asking about directly before ordering.

### Check tracking once you've ordered

If you do order, the tracking number provided should resolve through Royal Mail's or the stated UK courier's own tracking system, showing a UK originating depot. A tracking number that only works through an international tracking aggregator, or that shows an overseas origin, confirms the parcel isn't shipping from the UK.

## Ask directly if it's unclear

If the delivery policy doesn't state a dispatch location, email and ask directly where orders ship from. A UK-based seller can answer this immediately and specifically. A vague answer, or one that avoids naming a country, is itself informative.

### Companies House as a secondary check

A UK-registered company with an active Companies House entry and a UK registered office is a reasonable secondary signal the business itself is UK-based, though it doesn't guarantee the warehouse and dispatch point are in the same country. Use it alongside the delivery policy check, not instead of it.

## What UK dispatch looks like done properly

A seller such as Enzo Meds states UK dispatch and realistic UK delivery timeframes directly in its delivery policy, using Royal Mail Tracked services, which is the standard worth expecting from any seller claiming to be UK-based.

## Check for import or customs language

A genuinely UK-based seller shipping domestic UK orders has no reason to mention customs declarations, import duties, or clearance times anywhere in their standard delivery policy for UK addresses. If this language appears for what's supposed to be a domestic order, that's a clear indication the parcel is actually crossing an international border before it reaches you.

### Consider what this means for cold-chain reliability

International shipping adds transit time, and every extra day in transit is a day the ice pack and insulated packaging have to keep working. A seller genuinely shipping domestically within the UK has a much easier job maintaining cold-chain integrity than one shipping from overseas, which is a practical reason UK dispatch matters beyond customs risk alone.

## In short

Check the delivery policy for a stated UK dispatch location and a named UK courier service, don't rely on the domain extension alone, and ask directly if it's unclear. Unrealistic international delivery windows or vague shipping language are the clearest signs a "UK" seller isn't actually dispatching from the UK.
