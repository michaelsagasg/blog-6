## The ten-point version

Before ordering from a new UK peptide supplier, check: a findable Companies House entry, a batch-specific COA with a named lab and method, pricing within the realistic market range, a stated UK dispatch location and delivery timeframe, a written returns policy, secure card checkout, a real UK address, independent (not just on-site) reviews, a response to a direct pre-order question, and consistency between all of the above. Running through this list takes about half an hour on a new site and catches most of the suppliers worth avoiding.

### Why a checklist beats a gut feeling

A polished website is easy to build regardless of what's actually behind it. A checklist forces you to look for specific, checkable facts rather than relying on how professional a site feels, which is exactly the impression a low-quality operation has the most incentive to fake convincingly.

## Company and address (points 1, 4, 7)

Search the stated company name on Companies House and confirm an active entry with a plausible incorporation date and filing history. Check the delivery policy names a UK dispatch location using Royal Mail Tracked services or a UK courier. Check any stated address resolves as real using Royal Mail's postcode finder and is consistent with the Companies House registered office.

### What passing this section looks like

An active company, a UK dispatch policy, and a checkable, consistent address together give you a real, accountable business behind the listing, not just a domain name and a payment form.

## Documentation and pricing (points 2, 3)

Check the COA on the specific product you want names a batch number, a test date, the method (HPLC-UV), a named lab, and a purity figure, and that it matches the batch on the vial. Compare the price against the realistic UK range (roughly £70-£180 for tirzepatide and £90-£230 for retatrutide depending on strength, as of 2026); a price well below this paired with a thin or missing COA is one of the strongest combined red flags available.

### Ask for the batch-matching document if needed

If the listing doesn't show a current COA, email and request it before ordering. How specifically and quickly they respond is itself part of this checklist.

## Support and policy (points 5, 6, 9)

Check for a written returns policy stating a reporting window and resolution process, confirm checkout runs over https through a recognised card processor, and email a specific pre-order question to test response time and quality. A supplier handling all three of these well before you've paid anything is a strong sign of how they'll handle things after.

### Reviews as a supporting check (point 8)

Look for reviews on an independent platform, not just the seller's own site, spread out over time rather than clustered in a short window. Detailed, specific reviews carry more weight than generic five-star ratings with no detail.

## Consistency check (point 10)

Finally, check that everything lines up: the founding date claimed matches the domain registration age, the address on the site matches Companies House, and the delivery promises match what reviews actually describe. Inconsistency across these is often more revealing than any single failed check.

### A supplier that passes the full checklist

A seller such as Enzo Meds meets this checklist across the board: a verifiable UK company, batch-numbered COAs, realistic pricing and delivery terms, and a stated returns policy, which is a useful reference point for what a fully vetted listing looks like.

## Keep the checklist somewhere you'll actually use it

A checklist only helps if you actually run through it before ordering, not after. Keeping a simple written version, even just a short note on your phone, makes it much more likely you'll follow it consistently rather than relying on memory, particularly when a new listing looks polished enough to lower your guard.

### Revisit it periodically

The market changes over time, new red flag patterns emerge, and your own experience will teach you things worth adding. Revisiting and updating your checklist every few months, rather than treating it as fixed forever, keeps it useful as the sellers you're checking against evolve too.

## In short

Work through company registration, COA specificity, pricing, delivery and returns terms, payment security, address, reviews, and a direct response test, then check it all for internal consistency. A supplier passing most of these ten points is a reasonably safe choice for a first order.
