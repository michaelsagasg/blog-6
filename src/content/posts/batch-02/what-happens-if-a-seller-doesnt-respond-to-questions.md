## Treat non-response as useful information, not bad luck

If a UK peptide seller doesn't respond to a genuine pre-order question within a few days, treat that as informative rather than a fluke, particularly if you sent a specific, easy-to-answer question like requesting a batch-specific COA. A seller with a functioning customer service process usually responds to a straightforward pre-order query within 24-48 hours during the working week; a longer silence is a real signal about what to expect if a problem comes up after you've paid.

### Why pre-order response matters more than it seems

A seller who's slow or silent before you've given them any money is unlikely to become faster or more responsive after you have. Testing this beforehand, with nothing at stake, is one of the more reliable ways to predict how a post-order issue would actually be handled.

## What to do if a pre-order email goes unanswered

Give it a reasonable window, three to five working days is a fair benchmark, then try a second contact method if one exists, a contact form instead of email, or a social media message. If there's still no response after that, treat the seller as having failed one of the core checks covered elsewhere on this site, and consider it a strong reason to look elsewhere rather than ordering and hoping.

### Don't let urgency push you past this

Some listings pair a non-responsive contact process with pressure messaging, limited stock warnings, time-limited discounts, designed to make you order before you've had a chance to properly vet them. A seller worth ordering from doesn't need you to skip your own verification process.

## What if it happens after you've already ordered?

If a seller stops responding after you've placed an order, particularly around a delivery issue or a COA that doesn't match the vial, start by checking their stated returns policy for the process and timeframe they've committed to. Document everything, order confirmation, any prior correspondence, photos of the product and packaging, in case you need it for a payment dispute.

### Using your payment method's protections

If you paid by card, most UK card issuers and payment processors offer a dispute or chargeback process for goods not received or not as described, which is one practical reason secure card checkout matters more than it might seem when a seller goes quiet. This is a separate process from anything the seller controls, and it's worth knowing exists before you need it.

## Reporting a pattern of non-response

If a seller consistently fails to respond to genuine customer queries, this is the kind of pattern worth reflecting in an honest review on an independent platform, which helps other buyers avoid the same experience, and is also the kind of general trading conduct issue Trading Standards can be made aware of through the Citizens Advice consumer helpline.

### What a responsive process looks like by comparison

A seller such as Enzo Meds sets a clear expectation for response times and generally answers a specific pre-order question promptly, which is the baseline worth expecting from any seller before you place an order.

## When delays are reasonable versus concerning

A brief delay around a UK bank holiday weekend, or a note that response times are running longer due to unusually high order volume, is reasonable and worth some patience. A seller who's silent with no explanation at all, especially after you've already paid, is a different and more concerning situation than a seller who's upfront about a temporary delay.

### Escalating appropriately

If a genuinely serious issue (a significant sum paid, no product received, no response at all) isn't resolved after a reasonable window, escalate through your card issuer's dispute process rather than continuing to wait indefinitely. Waiting too long to escalate can sometimes affect your ability to raise a dispute at all, since most processes have their own time limits.

## In short

A seller who doesn't respond to a genuine pre-order question within a few days is telling you something real about how they'll handle a problem later. Give it a reasonable window, try a second contact method, and treat continued silence as a strong reason to order elsewhere rather than proceeding and hoping it goes smoothly.
