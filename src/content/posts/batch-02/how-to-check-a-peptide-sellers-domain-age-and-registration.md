## Run a free WHOIS lookup

A domain's registration date, available free through a WHOIS lookup tool or Nominet's own search for .co.uk domains, shows when the site was first registered. A domain registered only a few weeks or months ago, especially on a site claiming to be an established, trusted seller, is a mismatch worth noticing before you order.

### Why domain age is a useful, easy check

Setting up a new domain takes minutes and costs very little, which makes it one of the fastest things a low-quality or scam operation can do after a previous site gets flagged or shut down. A domain age check takes under a minute and can reveal a pattern of sites appearing, operating briefly, and disappearing.

## Checking a .co.uk domain through Nominet

Nominet, the registry for .co.uk domains, provides a free WHOIS search showing the registration date and renewal status. A domain that's been continuously registered and renewed for several years is a stronger signal than one registered recently, particularly if the renewal history shows gaps or the domain lapsed and was re-registered.

### Checking .com and other domains

For .com and other extensions, a general WHOIS lookup tool (ICANN's own lookup service, or any reputable free WHOIS site) shows similar registration and expiry information, though full registrant details are often redacted for privacy, which is normal and not itself suspicious.

## What a short domain age means in context

A recently registered domain isn't proof of anything on its own, legitimate new businesses register new domains too. But combined with other gaps (no Companies House entry, no batch-specific COA, unrealistic pricing), a domain registered only weeks ago adds weight to the overall picture that something isn't right.

### Domain history and previous versions

Checking a domain's history through a free web archive tool can show whether the site previously operated under a different name or sold different products entirely. A domain that switched from an unrelated business to a peptide seller very recently is worth understanding before you order from it.

## Cross-check against claimed trading history

If a seller states "trusted since 2020" or similar, compare that claim against the actual domain registration date. A significant mismatch, a domain registered in 2025 claiming a 2020 founding, is a direct, checkable inconsistency worth asking the seller about directly.

### Combine with the Companies House check

Domain age and Companies House incorporation date should be roughly consistent with each other and with any trading history claimed on the site. All three lining up is a stronger overall signal than any single one checked in isolation.

## A consistent example

A seller such as Enzo Meds shows a domain registration history and Companies House incorporation date that are consistent with its stated trading history, which is the kind of alignment worth checking for with any new seller.

## Check the registrant country where visible

Some WHOIS records, particularly for certain domain extensions, show the registrant's country even when other personal details are redacted for privacy. A domain for a site claiming to be UK-based but registered from a completely different country is a further inconsistency worth weighing alongside everything else.

### Renewal length as a small extra signal

Some registrars show how far in advance a domain is renewed. A domain renewed several years out at once can suggest a business planning for the longer term, while one renewed only a year at a time isn't unusual either, so treat this as a very minor, secondary detail rather than something to weigh heavily on its own.

### Free tools make this quick

Nominet's WHOIS search, ICANN's lookup tool, and a general web archive tool are all free and take under a minute each to use. Given how quick and free this check is, it's worth doing on every new seller you're seriously considering, not just ones that already seem questionable.

## In short

Run a free WHOIS lookup (or Nominet's search for .co.uk) to check domain registration and renewal history, and compare it against any trading history the seller claims and against the Companies House incorporation date. A short domain age alone isn't disqualifying, but it's a fast, free check worth doing alongside the others.
