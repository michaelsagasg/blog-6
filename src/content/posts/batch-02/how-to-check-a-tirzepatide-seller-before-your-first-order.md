## Verify first, then start small

For a first tirzepatide order from a new UK seller, check the Companies House registration, request the batch-specific COA if it isn't already visible, and consider starting with a single, smaller vial rather than a bulk order, even if the per-unit price improves at higher quantities. A first order tells you as much about the seller's actual process as it does about the product.

### Why a cautious first order makes sense

Tirzepatide is one of the most searched and most sold peptides in the UK, which means the market includes both well-established sellers and a constant churn of new, unverified sites trying to capture search traffic. Starting cautiously with any seller you haven't ordered from before is a reasonable way to limit exposure while you build confidence.

## Check the company is real

Search the seller's stated company name on Companies House before ordering. A genuine, active entry with a plausible incorporation date and filing history is one of the fastest, highest-value checks you can do, and it takes only a couple of minutes.

### Address consistency

Compare the Companies House registered office with any address stated on the seller's own site. The two don't need to be identical, but they should be consistent with a genuine operation rather than contradictory or unfindable.

## Request the batch-specific COA

Check that the listing shows a COA naming the current batch number (for example TZ-250601-11), a test date, the method (HPLC-UV is standard), and a purity figure, typically 98% or above for a well-manufactured batch. If it isn't shown on the listing, email and ask for it before ordering; a seller with a real testing process usually replies within a day or two.

### Checking it once it arrives

Confirm the batch number on the physical vial matches the COA you were sent or shown, and check the purity figure looks like a genuine measurement (a specific figure like 98.6%) rather than a suspiciously round number that never varies.

## Start with a smaller order

Even where a bulk order offers a better price per unit, ordering a single vial first lets you evaluate the actual delivery experience, packaging quality, whether the ice pack held its temperature, how closely delivery time matched what was promised, before committing to a larger purchase.

### What to check on arrival

Check the vial's batch number against the COA, note the condition of the insulated packaging and ice pack, and compare the actual delivery time against what was stated at checkout. Report any mismatch to the seller promptly, both to resolve the specific issue and to see how they handle it.

## Let the first order inform future ones

A smooth first order, accurate documentation, on-time delivery, a vial that matches what was promised, is a reasonable basis for larger orders going forward. Any real problems at this stage are worth taking seriously before increasing order size or frequency with that particular seller.

### A seller set up for a straightforward first order

A seller such as Enzo Meds shows batch-numbered COAs and clear delivery terms directly on the listing, which reduces how much you need to chase down before ordering for the first time, a pattern worth looking for with any new seller.

## Keep a record from your first order

Save your order confirmation, the COA provided, and any photos of the delivery and packaging. This gives you a genuine baseline to compare against on future orders, whether the batch number changes as expected, whether documentation stays consistent, and whether delivery timing holds up over repeat purchases.

### If something goes wrong on the first order

A real problem on a first order, a batch mismatch, a warm parcel, an unresponsive seller when you raise it, is worth treating as decisive rather than a one-off inconvenience. How a seller handles a genuine first-order issue tells you more about whether to trust them with a larger order than almost anything else you could check beforehand.

## In short

Check Companies House, request the batch-specific COA before paying if it isn't already shown, and start with a smaller order to test the seller's actual process. This approach limits your exposure while you build confidence in a seller you haven't bought from before.
