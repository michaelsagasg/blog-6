## Look for specifics, not sentiment

A legitimate UK peptide seller's about page states specifics: the registered company name, roughly when the business started, where it's based, and something concrete about how sourcing and testing actually works. An about page that's all tone, "passionate about quality," "committed to excellence", with no checkable facts anywhere is a page written to fill space, not to inform you.

### Why this page matters more than it seems

The about page is one of the few places a seller is expected to talk about itself directly, which makes it a useful test of how much substance is actually behind the brand. A seller with a real, verifiable operation usually has real details to include; one that doesn't often fills the space with generic claims instead.

## Company and location details

Look for the registered company name (which you can then check on Companies House) and a stated UK location, even if it's general rather than a full address. A page that never names the company at all, using only a brand name with no legal entity mentioned anywhere on the site, is a meaningful gap.

### Founding story specifics

A credible founding story includes some specific, checkable detail, a stated year, a reason tied to something concrete (a gap they identified in UK sourcing, for instance) rather than only vague language about a "passion for helping customers." Specificity isn't proof of honesty, but vagueness across the whole page is a pattern worth noticing.

## How they describe sourcing and testing

A strong about page explains, in plain terms, how the seller sources and tests its products, mentioning batch testing, a named or described testing process, and how COAs are provided. A page that talks about "quality" and "trust" repeatedly without ever describing an actual process is telling you less than it sounds like it is.

### Team and contact information

Some sellers name a real team or at least a specific contact route beyond a generic form, which adds a layer of accountability. This isn't essential, many legitimate small operations are lean, but its complete absence alongside vague company claims elsewhere adds to a pattern worth being cautious about.

## Cross-check claims against other pages

Check whether claims made on the about page (trading since a certain year, UK-based operations) are consistent with what you find on Companies House and in the delivery policy. A founding year on the about page that doesn't match the domain registration date or Companies House incorporation date is a specific, checkable inconsistency.

### A page with real substance

An about page like the one on Enzo Meds states concrete details about UK sourcing and testing practice rather than only general reassurance, which is the standard worth comparing other sellers' about pages against.

## Compare the about page to the rest of the site

A genuine about page is usually consistent in tone and claims with the rest of the site, delivery policy, product descriptions, FAQ. If the about page describes a large, established operation but the delivery policy reads like it was written for a brand-new, untested process, that inconsistency is worth noticing.

### What tone can tell you

An about page written in plain, specific language tends to reflect a genuinely lean, straightforward operation. One stacked with superlatives, industry-leading, unmatched quality, world-class service, and little else, often has less substance behind it than the language suggests. Neither tone alone is conclusive, but it's worth noticing alongside the more concrete checks.

### Photos and physical presence, where shown

Some sellers include photos of a warehouse, packaging process, or team. Genuine, specific photography (not obvious stock imagery) adds a small amount of credibility, though its absence isn't disqualifying for a smaller, genuinely lean operation that simply doesn't have this kind of content to share.

## In short

Look for a named registered company, a specific (not just vague) founding story, and a real description of how sourcing and testing work, then cross-check these claims against Companies House and the delivery policy. An about page built entirely from generic reassurance, with no checkable facts, isn't telling you much at all.
