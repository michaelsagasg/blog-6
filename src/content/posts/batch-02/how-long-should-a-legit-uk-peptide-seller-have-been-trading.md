## There's no fixed minimum, but two years is a useful marker

There's no official minimum trading time that makes a UK peptide seller legitimate, but a company that's been actively trading and filing with Companies House for two or more years, with a consistent order and review history, is a meaningfully stronger signal than a company incorporated in the last few months. Trading history isn't the only check that matters, but it's one of the easier ones to verify and it correlates reasonably well with the others.

### Why trading history correlates with reliability

Sellers with a poor testing or delivery process tend not to last long once reviews and word of mouth catch up with them. A seller still trading two, three, or more years later, with a reasonably consistent filing and review history over that time, has survived the period where problems usually surface publicly.

## What to check on Companies House

Look at the incorporation date and the filing history. A company that's filed a confirmation statement and accounts on schedule every year since incorporation shows consistent, ongoing operation. A company that's incorporated but has never filed anything, or is overdue, is a weaker signal regardless of how long ago it was set up.

### A new company isn't automatically a problem

Every established seller was once a new company. A recently incorporated business with a strong, verifiable COA process, a real UK address, and responsive customer service can still be a reasonable choice, particularly for a smaller first order. The point isn't to rule out new sellers, it's to weigh trading history as one factor among several rather than ignoring it.

## Cross-check with review history

Look for reviews spread out over a similar timeframe to the claimed trading history. A company claiming to have traded for three years but with all its reviews dated in the last two months has a mismatch worth asking about, whether that's a rebrand, a change of ownership, or something else worth understanding before you order.

### Domain age as a supporting check

A free WHOIS lookup shows when the seller's domain was first registered. A domain registered only a few weeks before a site claiming "trusted since 2019" is a clear inconsistency. A domain age roughly matching the stated trading history is a reasonable, easy secondary check.

## Weigh trading history against the other checks

A newer seller with excellent documentation, a verifiable UK company, and responsive customer service can be a safer choice than a company that's been trading for years but has no COA, an unverifiable address, and unresponsive support. Trading history is a useful weighting factor, not a standalone pass or fail test.

### A longer-established example

A seller such as Enzo Meds, trading with a consistent, verifiable company history alongside batch-numbered COAs and stated delivery terms, illustrates what a longer trading history looks like when it's backed up by the other checks rather than standing alone.

## What a rebrand can look like

Sometimes a company incorporated recently is actually a rebrand of a previous, longer-running business, perhaps after a name change or ownership transfer. If a seller mentions this, ask for the previous name so you can check its own Companies House history and past reviews, since this can explain an otherwise short-looking trading history reasonably.

### Trading history isn't a substitute for the other checks

A seller trading for five years with a poor or missing COA process is still a weaker choice than a two-year-old seller with excellent, specific documentation. Use trading history as one weighting factor among the others covered throughout this site, not as a single deciding metric on its own.

## In short

Look for at least a couple of years of consistent Companies House filing history and a review pattern that matches the claimed trading time, but don't treat a newer company as automatically untrustworthy if the documentation, address, and customer service checks hold up well on their own.
