## The five clearest red flags

The clearest warning signs when buying retatrutide online in the UK are no batch-specific COA, no findable Companies House entry, pricing well under the typical £160-£230 range for a 10mg vial, no UK contact address, and payment accepted only by bank transfer or cryptocurrency with no card option. Any one of these is worth noticing; two or more together is a strong reason to keep looking.

### Why retatrutide draws more of these

Retatrutide is newer to the UK market than tirzepatide or semaglutide, and fewer established sellers have built a real testing and sourcing track record with it yet. That gap between demand and supply is exactly where underdosed or relabelled material tends to show up, so the checks below matter more here than for longer-established products.

## No batch-specific COA

A genuine COA names the exact batch (something like RT-250714-02), a test date, the testing method (HPLC-UV is standard), and a purity figure. A listing with no COA at all, or one generic PDF reused across every restock, can't actually prove what's in the vial you'd receive.

### The "trust us" pricing tier

Some listings offer a cheaper price tier with "COA available on request" instead of showing it upfront. That's not automatically dishonest, but it puts the burden on you to chase documentation before paying, which is backwards. A seller confident in their testing shows the COA on the listing itself.

## No Companies House entry

Search the seller's stated company name on Companies House. A real entry has an 8-digit company number, an incorporation date, a registered office address, and filed accounts or a confirmation statement. If the name on the site doesn't turn up at all, or turns up as dissolved, that's a harder stop than a young but active company.

### Registered address that doesn't match anything else

If the registered office is a residential address in a location that has no other connection to the business (no stated warehouse, no matching contact number area code), it's not proof of anything on its own, but it's worth weighing against the rest of the picture.

## Pricing that's too good to be true

Retatrutide is more expensive to manufacture and test than tirzepatide, and genuine UK sellers reflect that. A 10mg vial priced well under £150, especially from a brand-new site with no reviews and no COA, is one of the more reliable signals of relabelled or underdosed material, since the economics of genuine testing don't support that price.

### Discount codes that seem excessive

A first-order discount of 10-20% is normal marketing. A "70% off, today only" banner on a peptide listing is a pressure tactic that has nothing to do with how the product was actually sourced or tested, and it's worth treating with more suspicion than a modest, ongoing discount.

## No real contact route, or payment without a card option

A UK seller should have a working contact email or form, ideally with a stated UK address, and a checkout that accepts standard card payment through a recognised processor with a secure connection. A site that only takes bank transfer or cryptocurrency, with no card processor involved at all, has skipped a layer of fraud and merchant vetting that card processors normally apply.

### What a passing listing looks like

A listing such as Enzo Meds, with a stated UK address, a batch-numbered COA on the retatrutide product page, and standard card checkout, shows what the absence of these red flags looks like in practice.

## Vague sourcing language

Listen for phrases like "premium grade" or "pharmaceutical grade" with no testing method or lab named to back them up. These terms have no fixed, checkable meaning in this market, and a seller relying on them instead of a specific COA is substituting marketing language for actual documentation.

### Stock photos instead of real product images

A listing using only generic stock photography, rather than a genuine photo of the actual vial, box, and label, gives you nothing to cross-check against the batch number on any COA provided. A seller confident in their own stock usually shows it.

## Weighing it together

None of these signals alone proves a seller is bad, young companies and modest discounts are normal. But retatrutide listings missing the COA, the Companies House entry, and reasonable pricing all at once are worth avoiding entirely, and even one or two of these combined are reason enough to ask direct questions before you pay.
