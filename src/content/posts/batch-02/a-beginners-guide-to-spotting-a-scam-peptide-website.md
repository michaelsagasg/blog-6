## Four checks that matter most for a first-timer

If you're new to buying peptides in the UK, focus on four checks before anything else: does the company exist on Companies House, does the listing show a batch-specific COA, is the price roughly in line with the market, and does checkout use secure card payment rather than bank transfer or crypto only. These four catch the large majority of outright scam sites without needing deep familiarity with the market.

### Why start with just four

It's easy to feel overwhelmed trying to check everything at once as a beginner. These four checks are the fastest to do, the hardest for a scam site to convincingly fake all at once, and together they cover company legitimacy, product legitimacy, and payment safety.

## Check one: does the company exist?

Go to the Companies House website and search the company name stated on the seller's site (check the footer or terms page if it's not obvious). A real, active company has an 8-digit company number, a registered office address, and an incorporation date. If nothing comes up at all, that's one of the clearest signs to walk away.

### What if no company name is given at all?

If the site never states a company name anywhere, that alone is a serious gap for a beginner to notice, since it means this first check can't even be attempted.

## Check two: is there a real COA?

Look for a certificate of analysis on the product page showing a specific batch number, a test date, a testing method (HPLC-UV is standard), and a purity figure. A listing with no COA at all, or only a vague "lab tested" claim with nothing attached, hasn't shown you anything you can actually verify.

### A simple rule of thumb

If you can't find a batch number anywhere on the listing, that's reason enough on its own to be cautious, regardless of how professional the rest of the site looks.

## Check three: does the price make sense?

As a rough guide for 2026, tirzepatide commonly costs £70-£180 per vial and retatrutide £90-£230 per vial from established UK sellers, depending on strength. A price dramatically below this range, especially paired with urgent "limited time" discount messaging, is one of the clearest scam signals for a beginner to learn to recognise.

### Trust the pattern, not just one low price

A single low-priced item isn't automatic proof of a scam, but a whole site priced well under the market with a missing COA and no findable company is a strong combined signal.

## Check four: is checkout secure?

Look for the https padlock in the browser bar and a card payment option through a recognised processor. A site that only accepts bank transfer or cryptocurrency, with no card option at all, has skipped an important layer of fraud protection that a beginner especially benefits from having in place.

### What a passing site looks like

A site such as Enzo Meds passes all four checks: a findable UK company, a batch-numbered COA on its listings, market-consistent pricing, and secure card checkout, which is a useful reference for what these four checks look like when everything's in order.

## Once you're comfortable, add more checks

As you get more familiar with the market, it's worth building up to the fuller set of checks covered elsewhere on this site, domain age, social media consistency, delivery policy specifics, review platform checks. The four covered here are a strong starting point for a beginner, not a ceiling on how thorough you can eventually be.

### Don't rush a first order

Even once a seller passes these four checks, there's no need to rush into a large first order. Starting with a single, smaller vial while you're still building confidence in a new seller is a sensible habit for any beginner, regardless of how well the site checks out on paper.

## In short

As a beginner, check the company on Companies House, look for a batch-specific COA, compare the price to the realistic market range, and confirm secure card checkout. These four checks alone will filter out most outright scam sites before you've spent a penny.
