## Check for a recognised processor and a secure connection

A genuinely secure checkout runs over https (a padlock icon in the browser bar, no "not secure" warning) and processes cards through a recognised payment processor, either directly integrated or via redirect to the processor's own page. If the checkout has no padlock, or asks for card details on a page with no visible security indicator, stop before entering anything.

### Why the processor matters, not just the padlock

An https padlock only confirms the connection is encrypted; it says nothing about whether the business behind the site has been vetted. A recognised card processor (Stripe, Shopify Payments, and similar widely used platforms) runs its own merchant vetting and fraud checks before allowing a business to process payments through it, which is a real, independent layer of scrutiny beyond encryption alone.

## Look at where you're redirected during checkout

Many legitimate sites redirect briefly to the payment processor's own domain to collect card details, rather than handling card entry directly on the seller's own page. Seeing the URL change to a recognised processor's domain during the actual card entry step is a normal and often reassuring part of a properly integrated checkout.

### What a poorly secured checkout looks like

A checkout collecting full card details on a page with no https padlock, no recognisable processor branding anywhere, and no redirect at all to a third-party payment page is a weaker setup. This doesn't automatically mean fraud, but it does mean the payment step hasn't been through the same vetting a processor-backed checkout has.

## Check what happens after you pay

A secure, properly integrated checkout gives an order confirmation with a reference number immediately after payment, usually followed by a confirmation email. A checkout that completes with no confirmation number at all, or where you're unsure whether the payment actually went through, is a weaker experience worth being cautious about, particularly for a first order.

### Look for alternative payment options too

A seller offering standard card payment alongside other options (and not only bank transfer or cryptocurrency) has generally been through processor vetting for the card option specifically. If card payment isn't offered at all, and only bank transfer or crypto is available, that vetting step has been skipped entirely.

## Consider a smaller first order to test it

If everything looks right but you're still uncertain, placing a smaller first order is a reasonable way to test the full payment and confirmation process before committing to a larger purchase, particularly with a new or less-established seller.

### An example of a properly secured setup

A checkout like the one used by Enzo Meds runs over a secure connection through a recognised card processor with a clear order confirmation, which reflects the baseline standard worth expecting before entering payment details anywhere.

## Check for a visible payment processor logo

Many sites display the logos of the payment methods they accept (Visa, Mastercard, and the processor itself) near the checkout button or in the footer. While logos alone can be added to any page, their presence alongside an actual working, redirected checkout is a small additional confirmation that a real processor is genuinely integrated.

### What to do if you're still unsure

If you've checked the padlock, the processor, and the confirmation process and you're still uncertain, consider using a payment method with strong buyer protection, most UK debit and credit cards offer some form of dispute process for goods not received or not as described, as a practical safety net for a first order with a new seller.

## In short

Check for the https padlock, look for a recognised card processor during checkout (often via a redirect), and confirm you receive a clear order confirmation afterwards. A checkout missing these basics, or offering only bank transfer or cryptocurrency with no card option, hasn't been through the vetting a properly integrated payment setup provides.
