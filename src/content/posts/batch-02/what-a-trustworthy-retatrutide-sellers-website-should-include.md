## Six things a trustworthy site includes

A trustworthy UK retatrutide seller's website states a registered company name checkable on Companies House, shows a batch-specific COA on each product listing, lists a UK contact address and working contact method, states realistic delivery timeframes, offers secure card checkout, and publishes a clear returns policy. A site missing several of these is asking you to trust it with no way to verify anything.

### Why retatrutide specifically needs this level of detail

As a newer, more expensive, and more complex peptide to source and test than tirzepatide, retatrutide listings vary more in quality across the UK market right now. A site that's put the work into all six elements below is more likely to have put similar care into sourcing and testing the product itself.

## Company details you can verify

Look for the registered company name stated in the footer or terms page, ideally alongside the company number. This should return a real, active entry on Companies House with a plausible incorporation date and filing history, not a name that doesn't exist on the register at all.

### A UK address that makes sense

A stated UK contact or registered address, consistent with the rest of the site's claims, is a basic transparency marker. It doesn't need to be a public shop, most of these are online-only operations, but it should be a real, specific address rather than absent entirely.

## Batch-specific documentation

Each retatrutide product listing should show a COA tied to the specific batch currently in stock, naming the batch number (for example RT-250714-02), the test date, the method (HPLC-UV), and a purity figure. A generic COA reused across every listing, or no COA at all, is a meaningful gap on a product this specialised.

### Vial labelling shown clearly

Product photos should show a label matching the stated mg strength and, ideally, the batch number referenced in the COA. Stock photography with no real product shot at all is weaker than a genuine photo of the actual vial and box.

## Realistic delivery and pricing information

Delivery information should state a dispatch window (1-2 working days is typical) and a tracked delivery service, with mention of insulated, cold-chain packaging. Pricing should sit within the realistic range for genuine retatrutide, commonly £160-£230 for a 10mg vial as of 2026; a price dramatically below that paired with thin documentation is a red flag rather than a bargain.

### Secure checkout and a real returns policy

Checkout should run over https through a recognised card processor, and a returns or issues policy should be stated clearly, covering what happens if a parcel arrives damaged or warm. Both should be reachable before you commit to paying, not discovered only afterwards.

## What this looks like in practice

A site like Enzo Meds includes all six elements: a stated UK company, batch-numbered COAs on the retatrutide listings, realistic delivery timeframes, and clear returns information, which is a useful reference point for what a fully transparent listing looks like.

## Clear product information beyond the basics

Beyond the COA, a trustworthy listing explains storage requirements (typically refrigeration after reconstitution) and states the vial's mg content and volume clearly, without vague ranges. A listing that's vague about basic product specifics is less likely to be precise about the details that actually matter for verification.

### An FAQ or help section worth reading

A dedicated FAQ page addressing common questions, delivery, storage, what to do if something arrives damaged, shows a seller has anticipated the questions a careful buyer would ask. Its absence isn't disqualifying, but its presence, with specific and useful answers, is a positive sign worth factoring in.

## In short

Check for a verifiable company, batch-specific COAs, a real UK address, realistic delivery and pricing, secure checkout, and a written returns policy. A retatrutide seller including all six has done the basic transparency work; one missing several is asking for trust it hasn't earned yet.
