## Know what a realistic window looks like

A genuine UK peptide seller typically dispatches within 1-2 working days of an order and delivers within 2-4 working days UK mainland using a tracked Royal Mail or courier service, with insulated packaging and an ice pack for cold-chain items. A listing promising same-day delivery nationwide, or giving no delivery estimate at all, is worth extra scrutiny.

### Why the window matters for peptides specifically

Peptides are typically shipped cold-chain, meaning the ice pack and insulated packaging need to keep the product at a stable temperature for the whole journey. A seller genuinely managing this properly usually states realistic timeframes because they know exactly how long their packaging holds up, rather than promising unrealistic speed they can't actually control.

## Check the stated dispatch time

Look for how long after ordering the seller says they'll dispatch, ideally within 1-2 working days. A seller vague about dispatch ("processed promptly") without a specific timeframe is harder to hold accountable if an order sits unprocessed for a week.

### Cut-off times and weekends

A genuine delivery policy usually states an order cut-off time (for example, orders before 2pm dispatch same day) and whether weekend or bank holiday orders are processed on the next working day. If none of this is stated, ask directly before ordering something time-sensitive around cold-chain packaging.

## Check the tracked delivery service used

Royal Mail Tracked 24 or Tracked 48, or a named courier, should be stated, along with whether a tracking number is provided once the order ships. A seller offering no tracking at all on a temperature-sensitive product is a meaningful gap, since you'd have no way to confirm where a delayed parcel actually is.

### Insulated packaging and ice packs

Check whether the delivery policy specifically mentions insulated packaging and ice packs for cold-chain items, rather than treating peptides the same as any other small parcel. This detail, small as it sounds, is a reasonable signal that the seller understands what they're shipping.

## Compare the stated time to reviews

Cross-check the seller's stated delivery window against independent reviews mentioning actual delivery experience. A consistent gap, reviews describing week-long waits when the site claims 2-4 days, is a stronger signal than the stated policy alone.

### What to do if delivery runs over

A clear policy should state what happens if a delivery is significantly delayed, whether that's a tracking update, a replacement ice pack sent, or a refund option. If this isn't addressed anywhere, ask before ordering something where a delay could mean a warm parcel on arrival.

## An example of a stated policy

A listing such as Enzo Meds states its delivery timeframes directly on the site, including dispatch windows and tracked service used, rather than leaving buyers to guess or discover it only after ordering.

## Consider the time of year

Delivery times can run slightly longer around UK bank holidays and the December period due to general courier volume, not anything specific to the seller. A realistic delivery policy sometimes notes this directly. If you're ordering around one of these periods, it's worth checking for any seasonal delay notice on the site before assuming a longer wait reflects a problem with the seller.

### Regional delivery differences

Delivery to more remote parts of the UK, the Scottish Highlands and Islands, for instance, can genuinely take a day or two longer than the standard mainland window even with a reliable courier. A seller who mentions this distinction directly is showing a more accurate, thought-through delivery policy than one quoting a single blanket timeframe for the whole UK.

## In short

Check for a specific dispatch window (1-2 working days), a tracked delivery service, and mention of insulated cold-chain packaging. Cross-reference the stated timeframe against independent reviews, and treat vague or missing delivery information as a gap worth asking about before you order.
