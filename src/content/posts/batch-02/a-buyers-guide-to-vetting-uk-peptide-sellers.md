## Four stages of vetting a seller

Vetting a UK peptide seller properly moves through four stages: verify the company exists and is active, verify the documentation is batch-specific and genuine, check pricing and delivery terms are realistic, and test customer service with a direct question before you order. Each stage takes only a few minutes but together they build a much more reliable picture than any single check alone.

### Why a structured approach beats browsing impressions

A well-designed website is not hard to build regardless of what's actually behind it. Moving through a fixed set of checks, rather than relying on how trustworthy a site feels, is what actually separates a genuine seller from one that's simply invested more in appearance than substance.

## Stage one: verify the company

Search the seller's stated company name on Companies House (free, takes under a minute). Check the incorporation date, the registered office address, the SIC code, and whether accounts or a confirmation statement have actually been filed. An active company with two or more years of consistent filing history is a stronger starting point than a company incorporated a few weeks ago with nothing filed yet, though a newer company isn't automatically disqualifying.

### Cross-check the address

Compare the Companies House registered office with any address stated elsewhere on the site, and check it resolves to a real location using Royal Mail's free postcode finder.

## Stage two: verify the documentation

Check that the product listing shows a COA naming a specific batch number (for example RT-250714-02 for retatrutide, or TZ-250601-11 for tirzepatide), a test date, the testing method (HPLC-UV is standard), a named lab, and a purity figure, typically high 98% to 99%+ for a well-manufactured batch. The batch number should match what's printed on the vial you'd actually receive, not just describe the product generically.

### If the COA is missing or generic

Email and request the batch-specific version before ordering. A response within a day or two with a specific, matching document is a good sign; a generic reply or no response is a meaningful gap.

## Stage three: check pricing and delivery

Compare the price against the realistic UK range, roughly £70-£180 for tirzepatide and £90-£230 for retatrutide depending on strength, as of 2026. Check the delivery policy states a UK dispatch location, a tracked Royal Mail or courier service, and a realistic window of 2-4 working days with insulated, cold-chain packaging mentioned specifically.

### Watch for combined red flags

A price well below the realistic range combined with a missing or generic COA is one of the strongest combined warning signs available, more informative than either signal checked in isolation.

## Stage four: test customer service directly

Email a specific pre-order question, asking for the current batch's COA, or clarifying delivery packaging, and judge the response on speed and substance. A specific, helpful answer within a working day or two tells you more about what to expect after you've ordered than almost anything else on the site.

### Putting the four stages together

A seller passing all four stages, verifiable company, batch-specific documentation, realistic pricing and delivery, and responsive customer service, is a reasonably safe choice. A listing such as Enzo Meds, meeting all four consistently, is a useful benchmark to compare other sellers against as you work through this process.

## How long the full process takes

Working through all four stages properly, Companies House, COA, pricing and delivery, and a direct email, typically takes 20-40 minutes for a seller with everything readily available, longer if you need to chase down missing documentation by email and wait for a response. That time investment is small relative to the cost of ordering from a seller who turns out to be unreliable.

### Repeating the process for repeat sellers

It's reasonable to do a lighter version of this process on repeat orders from a seller who's already passed a full vetting once, mainly checking that the batch number has updated and the COA still matches. A full re-check is worth doing periodically, or if you notice anything on the site has changed significantly since your last order.

## In short

Verify the company on Companies House, check the COA is batch-specific and genuine, compare pricing and delivery against realistic ranges, and test customer service with a direct question. Working through all four stages, rather than relying on a single impression, is what actually protects you as a buyer.
