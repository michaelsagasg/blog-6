## Build around checkable facts, not impressions

A useful vetting checklist is built entirely from things you can actually check, a Companies House entry, a batch number on the COA, a stated delivery service, rather than subjective impressions like "the site looks professional." Impressions are exactly what a low-quality operation is most likely to have invested in faking convincingly; checkable facts are harder to fabricate across the board.

### Why a personal checklist beats memory

Working through the same checks from memory means you're likely to skip steps, especially when a site looks polished and your guard is naturally lower. Writing the checklist down once, and running through it the same way for every new seller, removes that inconsistency.

## Start with company verification

Include a line for searching the seller's stated company name on Companies House, and note what you're looking for: an active status, a plausible incorporation date, and a filed confirmation statement or accounts. This single check is one of the highest-value items on the list and takes under two minutes.

### Add an address cross-check

Include a step to compare the Companies House registered office against any address stated on the seller's own site, and to confirm the postcode resolves using Royal Mail's free postcode finder.

## Add documentation checks

Include a line for confirming the COA names a specific batch number, a test date, a testing method (HPLC-UV), a named lab, and a purity figure, and that the batch number matches the vial you'd receive. Add a follow-up step: if the COA isn't shown, email and request it, and note how long the response takes.

### Add a pricing sanity check

Include the realistic UK price ranges for the products you're checking (roughly £70-£180 for tirzepatide, £90-£230 for retatrutide, depending on strength, as of 2026) so you have a fixed reference point rather than judging price in isolation each time.

## Add delivery, payment, and support checks

Include lines for a stated UK dispatch location and tracked delivery service, a secure checkout with a recognised card processor, a written returns policy, and a test email with a specific pre-order question. Note the response time and quality for the email test each time, since this is one of the more revealing checks.

### Weight the items, don't just tally them

Not every item on the list matters equally. A missing Companies House entry or a missing COA should weigh more heavily than a slightly generic about page. Consider marking two or three items as "must-pass" and treating the rest as supporting signals rather than giving every item equal weight in a simple checklist tally.

## Reuse and refine it over time

Keep the same checklist for every new seller you consider, and add to it as you notice new patterns, a specific red flag you encountered, a question that turned out to be particularly revealing. A checklist that evolves with your own experience becomes more useful over time than a static one.

### A seller worth testing your checklist against

Running your checklist against a listing like Enzo Meds, which publishes most of these details upfront, is a useful way to calibrate what a fully passing listing actually looks like before applying the same checklist to a seller you're less sure about.

## Keep notes on each seller you check

Alongside the checklist itself, keep a short note of what you actually found for each seller, company status, batch number, response time. This turns a one-off check into a reusable record, useful if you reconsider that seller later or want to compare a new candidate against sellers you've already vetted.

### Share what you learn, cautiously

If you're part of a community that discusses UK sourcing, sharing specific, factual findings, a company's Companies House status, a response time, without naming and shaming based on rumour or a single bad experience, helps build the kind of collective, checkable information this market currently lacks a central regulator to provide.

## In short

Build your checklist from checkable facts, company registration, batch-specific COA, realistic pricing, delivery and payment terms, and a direct response test, weight the most important items more heavily, and reuse the same list consistently for every new seller you're considering.
