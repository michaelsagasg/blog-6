## Look for a specific, written policy

A trustworthy UK peptide seller states a returns and issues policy in writing, on its own page or clearly linked from checkout, not just a vague "contact us if there's a problem" line. It should cover a reporting timeframe, what evidence is needed, and what resolution is offered.

### Why this matters more for peptides specifically

Cold-chain and quality issues (a warm parcel, a damaged vial, a mismatch with the COA) are more likely with peptides than with an ordinary consumer product, and a seller who hasn't thought through this in writing is less prepared to handle it well when it happens to you.

## What a clear policy states

Look for a stated reporting window, commonly 24 to 48 hours after delivery for damage or temperature issues, since cold-chain problems need reporting quickly to be assessed properly. It should also state what evidence is needed (photos of the parcel, the ice pack condition, the vial itself) and what resolution is offered, replacement or refund, if a genuine problem is confirmed.

### Vague policies to watch for

"Satisfaction guaranteed" with no further detail, or a policy that only says "email us" with no timeframe or evidence requirement stated, gives you nothing to hold the seller to if something goes wrong. A specific policy, even a strict one, is more useful than a vague generous-sounding one.

## Check whether it's actually accessible

The policy should be reachable from the checkout page or the footer without hunting through unrelated pages. If it's genuinely hard to find, or only appears after you've already paid (in a post-purchase email, for instance), that's a weaker setup than a policy stated clearly before you commit to buying.

### Confirm it applies to the product you're buying

Some general terms pages exclude certain categories from returns entirely. Check whether peptides or research chemicals are specifically excluded from any refund or replacement provision, since a generic "no returns on this category" line can undercut an otherwise reasonable-sounding policy elsewhere on the site.

## Test it with a real question

Before ordering, email a specific, genuine pre-order question, for example asking what happens if a parcel arrives without an ice pack still cold. How quickly and how substantively the seller answers is a reasonable proxy for how they'd handle an actual post-order issue, since it reflects the same customer service process.

### What a good response looks like

A specific answer referencing their actual policy (a stated timeframe, a named resolution) within a working day or two is a positive sign. A generic reply that doesn't address your specific question, or no reply at all after several days, tells you what to expect if something goes wrong with an order.

## Check exclusions carefully

Some returns policies exclude opened or used items from any refund, which is reasonable for a product like this, but should still allow for a report and resolution if the product itself was faulty, damaged, or didn't match its COA on arrival, before you've used it. A policy that excludes any claim once a parcel has been opened at all, with no exception for a faulty or mismatched product, is unusually restrictive.

### Look for a policy that's specific to peptides

A generic ecommerce returns policy copied from a template, with no mention of temperature-sensitive handling or COA mismatches at all, suggests less thought has gone into the process than a policy written specifically with this product category in mind.

## In short

Check that a returns policy exists in writing, that it states a reporting window and required evidence, and that it's reachable before checkout, not just discovered afterwards. Testing it with a real pre-order question is the most reliable way to see how a seller would actually behave if something went wrong.
