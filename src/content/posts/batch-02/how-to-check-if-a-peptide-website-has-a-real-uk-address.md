## Cross-check the address against two sources

A genuine UK address stated on a peptide seller's site should match, or at least be consistent with, the registered office address shown on Companies House, and should resolve to a real, deliverable location using Royal Mail's free postcode finder. If the address on the site doesn't match Companies House, or the postcode doesn't resolve to a real address at all, that's a concrete, checkable inconsistency.

### Why an address matters as a signal

A stated address that's real and checkable adds accountability that a contact form alone doesn't provide. It's not proof of product quality, a real address doesn't guarantee good testing practice, but a fabricated or non-existent address removes a basic layer of transparency that legitimate businesses don't typically avoid.

## Check the postcode with Royal Mail's postcode finder

Royal Mail provides a free postcode lookup tool that confirms whether a given postcode and address actually exist and are deliverable. A postcode that doesn't resolve at all, or resolves to a location clearly inconsistent with the stated address (an industrial estate claimed as a retail address with no such unit number, for instance), is worth further checking.

### Google Maps as a secondary check

Searching the address on a mapping service can show whether it's a residential property, a business park, a virtual office provider, or doesn't appear to exist at all. A virtual office or mailbox address isn't necessarily dishonest, many legitimate small online businesses use one, but it's worth knowing which kind of address you're dealing with.

## Compare against the Companies House registered office

If the seller is a UK limited company, its Companies House entry states a legal registered office address. This should be consistent with, though not necessarily identical to, any address stated elsewhere on the site (a warehouse or returns address can reasonably differ from the registered office). What matters is that neither address is fabricated or unfindable.

### A mismatch worth asking about

If the site states one UK city or region while the Companies House registered office is in a completely different, unrelated part of the country with no explanation, that's not automatically a problem, but it's worth a direct question if you're already uncertain about the seller for other reasons.

## Watch for addresses that are too vague to check

A site stating only "based in London" or "UK warehouse" with no specific address at all gives you nothing to verify directly. A specific street address, even a small unit number on an industrial estate, is checkable in a way a vague regional claim isn't.

### An example of a checkable address

A seller such as Enzo Meds states a specific UK address that's consistent with its Companies House registration, which is the standard of transparency worth expecting before you trust a seller with your payment details.

## What a virtual office address means

Virtual office and mailbox providers are legitimate services used by many small, genuine online businesses to maintain a professional business address without a physical shopfront. Recognising one isn't a red flag by itself, but it does mean the address check alone tells you less than it would for a genuine, standalone business premises, so weigh it more heavily alongside the Companies House and COA checks.

### Street View as a quick sanity check

Where a mapping service offers a street-level image, a quick look can confirm whether an address is a plausible business location, a residential street, an industrial estate, a serviced office building, rather than something that looks entirely inconsistent with any kind of business operating there at all.

## In short

Check the stated address against Royal Mail's postcode finder and against the Companies House registered office if the seller is a limited company. A specific, consistent, resolvable address is a meaningful transparency signal; a vague regional claim or a non-existent postcode is a gap worth investigating before you order.
