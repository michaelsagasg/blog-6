## Search the free Companies House register

Go to the Companies House website (find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk) and search the exact company name stated on the seller's site, usually found in the footer, terms and conditions, or delivery policy page. Every genuine UK limited company has an 8-digit company number, and the search is free and takes under a minute.

### What to do if the name isn't stated anywhere

If a seller's site never names a registered company at all, that's a more significant gap than a young company with little filing history. A sole trader doesn't need a Companies House entry, but should then state that clearly and provide another form of verifiable identity, such as a named individual and address.

## Read the incorporation date

The entry shows when the company was incorporated. A company that's been active for two or more years with a consistent filing history is a stronger signal than one incorporated a few weeks ago with nothing filed yet. A recent incorporation date isn't automatically a problem, businesses have to start somewhere, but it means you should lean more heavily on the other checks (COA, reviews, delivery practice) before ordering.

### Check the company status

The status field should say "Active." A status of "Dissolved," "Liquidation," or "Strike off action pending" on a company whose name matches a currently trading website is a serious mismatch worth investigating before you order anything.

## Check the SIC code

The Standard Industrial Classification (SIC) code describes what the company says it does. Retail sale via mail order or the internet is typically coded 47910; wholesale of pharmaceutical goods is 46460. A SIC code with no connection at all to retail, wholesale, or health products (for example, a code for construction or software) doesn't prove wrongdoing, but it's worth noticing as inconsistent with what the site claims to be.

### Filing history tells you about consistency

Click through to the filing history tab. A confirmation statement filed annually and accounts filed on schedule show a business meeting its basic legal obligations. Overdue filings or a large gap since the last filing are signals worth weighing, though not by themselves proof of anything about product quality.

## Match the registered address

The registered office address on Companies House should be a real, specific UK address. It doesn't have to be the warehouse the products ship from, many businesses use an accountant's or formation agent's address for this purpose, but it should be consistent with a genuine, ongoing business rather than appearing on lists of addresses used by dozens of unrelated shell companies.

### A quick cross-check

Searching the registered address alongside the word "company formation" can reveal whether it's a mass-registration address used by a formation agent for hundreds of unrelated businesses. That's common and not inherently suspicious, but it means the address check alone won't tell you much, and you should weigh it alongside the COA and delivery checks.

## What a solid entry looks like in practice

A seller like Enzo Meds stating a real UK company name that returns an active, multi-year Companies House entry with filings up to date is the kind of registration worth using as your baseline comparison for any other seller you're checking.

## Check for persons with significant control

Companies House also lists people with significant control (PSC) over the company, generally its owners or directors. A named individual with a consistent history across other filings can add a little more confidence than an entry with no PSC information at all, though many small, genuine businesses have simple, minimal PSC records that aren't cause for concern on their own.

### Look at related companies

If the same director or PSC appears linked to several other dissolved companies with short lifespans, that pattern is worth noticing, particularly if those companies operated in a similar space. It doesn't automatically mean anything about the current business, but it's a reasonable extra data point when you're already unsure.

## In short

Search the exact company name, confirm the status is Active, check the incorporation date and filing history for consistency, and note whether the SIC code and address are plausible. This whole check takes about five minutes and is one of the highest-value checks you can do before ordering from a new seller.
