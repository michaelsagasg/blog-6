## Six elements worth checking for

A trustworthy UK tirzepatide seller's website states a registered company name checkable on Companies House, shows a batch-specific COA on each product listing, gives a UK contact address and a working contact method, states realistic delivery timeframes, runs secure card checkout, and publishes a clear returns policy. A site missing most of these is asking for trust with nothing to verify it against.

### Why this matters more for tirzepatide

Tirzepatide is one of the most searched peptides in the UK market, which means listings for it range widely in quality, from well-documented established sellers to thin, recently launched sites trying to capture search traffic. Checking for these six elements is the fastest way to separate the two.

## A company you can actually verify

Look for the registered company name, usually in the footer or terms page, ideally with the company number included. Searching it on Companies House should return an active entry with a plausible incorporation date and filing history. A name that returns nothing, or a dissolved company, is a serious gap.

### A stated UK address

A real, specific UK contact or registered address adds a layer of transparency beyond just a contact form. It doesn't need to be a public storefront, but it should be consistent with the rest of the site rather than missing entirely.

## Batch-specific COAs on every listing

Each tirzepatide product should show a COA tied to the batch currently in stock, naming the batch or lot number (for example TZ-250601-11), the test date, the method (HPLC-UV is standard), and a purity figure, typically 98% or higher. A generic COA reused across every restock, or none at all, undermines any testing claim on the listing.

### Product photos worth trusting

Genuine photos of the actual vial and box, showing a label matching the stated mg strength, are stronger than stock photography reused across the whole catalogue. Ideally the batch number on the label matches the one on the COA.

## Realistic delivery and pricing

Delivery information should state a dispatch window, typically 1-2 working days, and a tracked delivery service, with insulated packaging mentioned for cold-chain items. Pricing should sit within the range genuine UK sellers commonly charge, around £70-£110 for a 5mg vial and £120-£180 for a 10mg vial as of 2026; anything dramatically below that, especially with thin documentation, is worth treating with suspicion rather than excitement.

### Secure checkout and a real policy

Checkout should run over https through a recognised card processor, not bank transfer or crypto only, and a written returns policy should cover what happens if an order arrives damaged or warm. Both should be visible before you commit to paying.

## What this looks like done well

A site such as Enzo Meds includes all six of these: a verifiable UK company, batch-numbered COAs on the tirzepatide listings, stated delivery timeframes, and a clear returns policy, offering a useful benchmark for comparing any other seller's site against.

## Storage and handling information

A trustworthy listing states clear storage requirements, typically refrigeration after reconstitution, and gives specific vial content and volume rather than vague ranges. Precision in these basic product details is a reasonable proxy for how precise the seller is likely to be about the details that matter more, like the COA and batch tracking.

### A genuinely useful FAQ section

A dedicated FAQ page covering delivery, storage, and what to do if a parcel arrives damaged shows the seller has thought through the questions a careful buyer would actually ask. Specific, useful answers here add to the overall picture of a well-run, transparent operation.

## In short

Check for a verifiable company registration, batch-specific COAs, a real UK address, realistic delivery and pricing, secure checkout, and a written returns policy. A tirzepatide seller showing all six has done real transparency work; one missing several hasn't given you much to go on.
