## Look for the batch number first

A trustworthy tirzepatide listing states a specific batch or lot number, for example TZ-250601-11, either directly on the product page or on the attached COA, and that number should match what's printed on the vial you actually receive. Without it, any testing claim on the listing can't be tied to the specific product being shipped to you.

### The link between COA and vial

The batch number is what connects a lab report to a physical product. A seller could otherwise show a genuine, well-tested COA for one batch while shipping a different, untested one. Checking that the numbers match takes seconds and closes off one of the easiest ways a listing can mislead without technically lying.

## What a real batch number looks like

Genuine batch numbers usually follow a consistent internal format, often a product code, a date, and a sequence number. They change between manufacturing or repackaging runs, meaning a seller restocking regularly should show different batch numbers over time, not the same one for months on end.

### A static batch number is a warning sign

If a seller has clearly restocked (the item went from "out of stock" back to available, or reviews reference recent purchases) but the batch number on the listing hasn't changed in that time, it suggests the number isn't being updated to reflect actual inventory, which undermines its value as a verification tool.

## Where the number should appear

Check for the batch number in three places: the product listing itself, the COA document, and the vial label as shown in product photos. Consistency across all three is what makes it useful. A number that only appears buried in a COA PDF, and nowhere on the listing page itself, is easy to overlook and easy for a seller to fudge.

### What to do if it's missing

Email the seller and ask which batch is currently shipping and for the matching COA. A response within a working day or two that includes a specific batch number and a corresponding lab report is what a genuine per-batch process looks like. Vague replies, or a COA that doesn't actually name a batch, are worth treating as a real gap.

## Why this check matters for tirzepatide

Because tirzepatide is heavily searched and heavily sold in the UK, batch turnover should be relatively frequent for an established seller with real ongoing stock. A seller claiming high sales volume but showing a batch number that never changes is a mismatch worth noticing.

### A working example

A listing like Enzo Meds shows the batch number directly on the tirzepatide product page, matching the number on the attached COA, which is the pattern worth checking any other tirzepatide seller against.

## Comparing batch numbers across repeat orders

If you order from the same seller more than once, compare the batch numbers between orders. A new, sequential-looking number on a repeat purchase suggests genuine stock turnover. Given how quickly tirzepatide typically sells given the demand, the same batch number showing up across orders placed months apart is a pattern worth asking the seller about directly.

### Why this is worth the small effort

None of this takes long once it's a habit, checking the listing, matching it to the COA, and jotting down the number takes under five minutes per order. Given how much it narrows down what you're actually trusting, it's one of the better returns on a small amount of effort available to a buyer in this market.

### Keeping your own order record

It's a good habit to keep a simple note of the batch number and COA for every tirzepatide order you place, alongside the order date. If a concern about a specific batch ever surfaces, in a review, a forum thread, or directly from the seller, your own record makes it easy to check whether it applies to you.

## In short

Check that a batch number is visible on the listing, confirm it matches the COA, and notice whether it changes over time as stock turns over. A seller who can't produce a specific, matching batch number, even when asked directly, hasn't given you anything to actually verify.
