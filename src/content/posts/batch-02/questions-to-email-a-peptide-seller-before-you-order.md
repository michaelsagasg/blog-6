## Six questions worth asking directly

Before ordering from a new UK peptide seller, email these six questions: which batch is currently shipping and can you see its COA, what UK company is behind the site, what delivery service and timeframe applies, how is cold-chain packaging handled, what's the returns process if something arrives damaged, and does checkout run through a card processor. The answers, and how quickly and specifically they come back, tell you as much as anything else you'll find on the site itself.

### Why asking directly works better than just reading the site

A website is written once and polished. An email reply is generated on the spot, in response to your specific question, which makes it much harder for a seller to fake convincingly. A good written policy combined with a good direct answer is the strongest combination; a good policy with a vague or absent answer is a mismatch worth noticing.

## Question 1: Which batch, and can I see the COA?

Ask specifically which batch number is currently shipping for the product you want, and request the matching COA if it isn't already on the listing. A seller with a genuine per-batch process answers this specifically, often attaching the document directly. A generic reply pointing back to a listing that doesn't actually show a batch number isn't a real answer.

### Question 2: What's the registered company name?

Ask for the legal company name behind the site if it isn't already stated. This lets you check Companies House yourself. A seller who won't provide this, or gives an evasive answer, has told you something important.

## Question 3: What's the delivery service and timeframe?

Ask specifically which courier or Royal Mail service is used and what dispatch and delivery times to expect. A specific answer (for example, "Royal Mail Tracked 24, dispatched within 1-2 working days") is stronger than a vague "shipped promptly."

### Question 4: How is cold-chain packaging handled?

Ask directly how the product stays cold in transit, insulated packaging, ice packs, and for how long these typically last in transit. A seller who understands their own cold-chain process answers this specifically; one who seems unsure suggests less attention to this detail than you'd want.

## Question 5: What happens if something arrives damaged?

Ask what the process is if a parcel arrives without a working ice pack, or a vial arrives damaged. A specific answer describing a reporting window and resolution (replacement or refund) is what a genuine returns process looks like in practice.

### Question 6: Does checkout accept card payment securely?

If it's not obvious from the checkout page itself, ask directly whether card payment is available through a recognised processor. This confirms the payment security check without you having to start a checkout to find out.

## Judging the response as a whole

A response within a working day or two that answers all six questions specifically is a strong positive signal. A response that ignores several questions, comes back a week later, or pushes you toward ordering rather than answering, tells you what future interactions with this seller are likely to be like.

### An example of a seller set up to answer these well

A seller such as Enzo Meds publishes most of these answers upfront (company details, delivery timeframes, batch-numbered COAs), which is itself a good sign, since it means less needs to be chased down by email in the first place.

## A bonus question worth asking

Ask how long they've been trading and whether they can point you to independent reviews beyond their own site. This tests both their transparency and gives you somewhere else to verify their claims. A seller happy to point you toward independent, unfiltered feedback is showing more confidence than one that only directs you back to their own testimonials page.

### Keep a written record of the exchange

Save the email thread once you've asked these questions. If an issue comes up later, having the seller's own written answers about their batch testing, company details, or delivery process on record is useful, both for resolving the issue directly and, if needed, for a payment dispute.

## In short

Ask about the batch and COA, the company name, delivery specifics, cold-chain handling, the returns process, and payment security, all before you order. How a seller answers these six questions is one of the most reliable tests available to you as a buyer.
