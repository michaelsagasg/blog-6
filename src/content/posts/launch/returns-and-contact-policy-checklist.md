## Why this policy matters here specifically

Given the cold-chain and quality concerns specific to peptides, a clear policy for what happens if an order arrives damaged, warm, or doesn't match its COA is more important than it might be for an ordinary consumer product.

### What a clear policy covers

A stated timeframe for reporting an issue, what evidence is needed (photos of the parcel, the ice pack condition, the vial itself), and what resolution is offered (replacement, refund) if a genuine problem is confirmed.

## Contact information you can actually verify

A working email address or contact form that gets a real response, rather than a form that appears to submit but never gets acknowledged, is worth testing with a genuine pre-order question before relying on it for a post-order issue.

### Response time as a signal

How quickly and how substantively a supplier responds to a pre-order question is a reasonable proxy for how they'd likely handle a post-order issue, since it reflects the same underlying customer service process.

## What to do if this information is missing

A listing or site with no visible returns policy and no clear contact route is a meaningful gap, worth asking about directly before ordering, not discovering only if something goes wrong.
