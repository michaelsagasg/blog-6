## Payment method options

A checkout that only accepts unusual payment methods, cryptocurrency only, bank transfer only, with no standard card processing available, is worth noticing. It's not automatically disqualifying, but standard card processors generally have their own fraud and legitimacy checks on merchants, which is a layer of scrutiny a cash-only or crypto-only checkout doesn't have.

### Secure connection basics

A checkout page should use a secure connection (the padlock icon, an https address). This is a baseline, not a strong signal of legitimacy on its own, but its absence on a page asking for payment details is a clear warning sign.

## Order confirmation and communication

A checkout that provides a clear order confirmation, with an order number and expected dispatch information, is behaving like an established business process. A checkout that gives no confirmation at all, or vague "we'll be in touch" messaging, is a weaker sign.

### Returns and contact information

Checking whether contact information and a returns or issues policy are genuinely accessible from the checkout flow, not just buried somewhere else on the site, is worth doing before completing a first order with a new seller.

## Weighing it together

None of these checks alone determines legitimacy, but a checkout that handles all of them professionally is a reasonable positive signal to add to the documentation and shipping checks covered elsewhere.
