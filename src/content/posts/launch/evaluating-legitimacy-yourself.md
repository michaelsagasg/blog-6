## Why "someone said it's legit" isn't enough

A single recommendation, whether from a forum post, a review, or a friend, reflects one person's experience with one order at one point in time. It's a useful data point, not a substitute for checking the specific things that actually establish legitimacy.

### What "legitimate" breaks down into

Documented, batch-specific product testing. Genuine cold-chain shipping practice. Transparent, verifiable business information. Consistent, responsive communication. Legitimacy isn't one property; it's the combination of several, each checkable somewhat independently.

## A simple evaluation process

Request a batch-specific COA before ordering and check it states a real lab and testing method. Ask directly about shipping and packaging practice. Look for independent reviews, not just on-site testimonials. Test responsiveness with a genuine pre-order question.

### Weighing mixed signals

A source that's strong on documentation but vague on shipping, or vice versa, isn't automatically disqualified, but it's a reason to ask more specific follow-up questions before ordering rather than assuming the strong signal covers the weak one.

## Making it a repeatable habit

Applying this same process to every new source you consider, rather than doing a thorough check once and then assuming all future orders from anywhere are equally safe, is what actually keeps you protected over time.
