## Where the label comes from

"Research chemical" or "research use only" labelling is common across this market and generally reflects that the product hasn't gone through the formal approval process required for it to be marketed as a licensed medicine in the UK. It's a regulatory and labelling distinction, not necessarily a comment on the manufacturing quality of any specific batch.

### Why this labelling exists

It reflects how the product is being sold and classified, separate from the medical licensing pathway that approved pharmaceuticals go through. A legitimate seller uses this labelling because it's accurate to the product's regulatory status, not as a loophole.

## What it doesn't tell you

The label alone says nothing about a specific batch's actual purity or whether it matches its stated content. That's exactly what a batch-specific COA is for, and why the labelling and the lab documentation are two separate things worth checking independently.

### Why both matter together

A product correctly labelled but with no COA backing it up leaves you with a regulatory disclosure but no actual verification of quality. A product with a COA but unclear labelling raises its own questions. Both together give a fuller picture than either alone.

## What to look for on a listing

Accurate, unambiguous labelling, a batch-specific COA available on request or upfront, and a seller willing to explain both clearly if asked.
