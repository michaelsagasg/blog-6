## Reliability has at least three separate parts

Product reliability (does the batch consistently match its stated content), delivery reliability (does it arrive on time and properly handled), and service reliability (does the seller respond consistently if something goes wrong). A supplier can be strong in one area and weak in another, which is why "reliable" as a single word can be misleading.

### Product reliability

This comes down to consistent, batch-specific lab testing across orders, not just a one-time favourable result. A single good COA doesn't establish consistency; a pattern across multiple orders, from your own experience or from independent reviews, does.

## Delivery reliability

Consistent dispatch times and cold-chain handling across multiple orders, not just a single successful delivery, is what actually indicates reliability here. A single good experience is a positive data point, not proof of consistency.

### Service reliability

How a supplier handles an actual problem, a delayed order, a documentation question, a damaged parcel, reveals more about service reliability than how smoothly things go when nothing goes wrong.

## Evaluating all three together

A genuinely reliable supplier tends to perform consistently across all three dimensions. Weighing them separately, rather than accepting a single positive impression as covering everything, gives a more accurate picture.
