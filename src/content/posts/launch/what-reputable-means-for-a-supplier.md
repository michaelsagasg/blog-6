## Reputation can be earned, or manufactured

A supplier's reputation can reflect years of consistent, verifiable practice, or it can reflect effective marketing and selectively published reviews. From the outside, the two can look similar at first glance, which is exactly why reputation alone isn't a reliable signal without digging into what it's actually built on.

### What an earned reputation looks like

Independent reviews across multiple, unconnected platforms, a track record that holds up over time rather than a burst of recent activity, and specifics (batch numbers, testing details) rather than vague praise are all signs of a reputation that's actually been earned through consistent practice.

## What a manufactured reputation can look like

A cluster of similar-sounding reviews posted in a short window, reviews only on channels the seller controls, or a reputation that seems to rest entirely on price rather than documented quality are all worth treating with more scepticism.

### Age of the business isn't a perfect proxy either

A longer-established seller has had more time to build a track record, but it's not a guarantee of ongoing quality; practices can change. Checking recent, not just historical, signals matters.

## Using reputation as one input, not the deciding factor

Reputation is worth factoring in alongside, not instead of, direct verification: your own check of a batch-specific COA and shipping practice for a specific order.
