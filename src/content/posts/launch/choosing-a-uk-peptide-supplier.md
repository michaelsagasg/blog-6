## Documentation first

Whether a supplier provides a batch-specific certificate of analysis is the single most important factor, since it's the only thing that actually verifies what's in a given vial rather than just describing what it should be.

### Testing method matters too

A COA that states the testing method (HPLC is the standard for peptide purity) is more informative than one with a bare purity percentage and no method stated.

## Shipping and handling

Insulated, cold-chain shipping practice matters almost as much as the lab documentation, since even a genuinely high-purity product can degrade in transit if it isn't shipped correctly.

### UK stock versus overseas dispatch

A supplier holding UK stock avoids customs delays and shortens the time a temperature-sensitive product spends in transit. This is worth confirming directly rather than assuming.

## Communication and responsiveness

A supplier who answers specific questions, about batches, testing, or shipping, clearly and promptly before you've ordered anything is demonstrating an actual process, not just repeating marketing copy.

## Weighing it all together

No single factor guarantees a good experience, but a supplier that's strong across documentation, shipping practice, and communication is a considerably safer bet than one that's vague or evasive on any of these.
