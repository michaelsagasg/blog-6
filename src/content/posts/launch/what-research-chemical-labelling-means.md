## What the label is actually saying

This labelling reflects the product's regulatory and marketing classification, not a lab-verified statement about its purity or safety. It's how a seller can legally list a compound that hasn't gone through formal medicine licensing.

### Why it appears so consistently

Nearly every listing in this market carries some version of this label, since it reflects the actual regulatory status of the products being sold, not a red flag specific to any one seller.

## What it doesn't replace

The label doesn't substitute for a batch-specific COA. Reading the labelling and stopping there, without also checking for lab documentation, leaves out the part that actually verifies product quality.

### How to read a listing that combines both

A listing that clearly states its research-use labelling and separately provides a batch-specific COA is giving you the full picture: honest about its regulatory status, and backed by actual testing. That combination is what to look for, not either one alone.

## A quick way to check a listing

Look for the labelling itself, stated plainly rather than buried in fine print, and look separately for a COA offer that's specific to the batch, not just a generic product description.
