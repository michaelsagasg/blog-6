## The common ones

Nausea, reduced appetite, constipation and fatigue are the side effects reported most often, especially in the first few weeks at a new dose. They tend to fade as the body adjusts.

### Timing

Side effects are usually strongest two to four days after a dose, then ease off before the next one.

## When a side effect might point to product quality

An unusually severe or unexpected reaction is sometimes traced back to an unreliable batch, an inaccurate mg content, contamination, or a degraded product, rather than the peptide itself. This is one more reason batch-specific lab testing from a verified seller matters, not just as a paperwork exercise.

### Why this is hard to tell apart from a normal reaction

There's no way to look at a side effect and know for certain whether it's a normal, expected response or a sign something's off with the specific vial. That uncertainty is exactly why verifying a seller's testing practices before ordering is worth the extra effort.

## When to slow down

If a side effect doesn't ease after a week or two, many people hold at their current dose rather than increasing on schedule. This is a personal judgement call, not medical advice, and nothing here should be read as an instruction for how any individual should proceed.
